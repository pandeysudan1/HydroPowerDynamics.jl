# Trollheim AGC — with vs without surge tank

This example studies a 75 MW -> 90 MW electrical load step in a Trollheim-inspired hydropower plant using **HydroPowerDynamics.jl**, ModelingToolkit.jl and OrdinaryDiffEq.jl.

The objective is deliberately narrow: keep the turbine, rotating mass, electrical load and controller identical, and change only the hydraulic topology by adding a surge-tank branch.

## Validated status

The model has been executed in GitHub Actions with Julia 1.11.9 using `Rodas5P`. Both nonlinear cases integrate successfully from the hidden conditioning interval through `t = 65 s`.

Validated workflow run:

## Topology

```text
Case A — without surge tank

Reservoir -> headrace -> penstock -> Francis turbine -> rotor -> generator/load
                                      ^                 |
                                      |                 v
                                      +------- AGC <- speed

Case B — with surge tank

Reservoir -> headrace -> junction -> penstock -> Francis turbine -> rotor -> generator/load
                         |                                      ^                 |
                         +-> surge tank                          |                 v
                                                               +------- AGC <- speed
```

The surge-tank junction is acausal:

```julia
connect(headrace.port_b, penstock.port_a, surge.port)
```

so the connector equations impose common pressure and mass-flow conservation.

## Disturbance and controller

The electrical disturbance is generated by the package-level `VariableLoadGenerator`:

```julia
generator.P_load.u ~ ifelse(t < 5.0, 75e6, 90e6)
```

The controller does **not** receive the load step as feed-forward information. It reacts to shaft-speed/frequency error only.

The new reusable `SimpleGovernorAGC` is

$$
e_f = \frac{\omega_{ref}-\omega}{\omega_{ref}},
$$

$$
\dot{\xi}=e_f,
$$

$$
u_{cmd}=u_0+\frac{e_f}{R}+K_i\xi,
$$

$$
T_g\dot u_g=\operatorname{sat}(u_{cmd})-u_g.
$$

For this benchmark,

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| Permanent droop $R$ | 0.50 pu/pu |
| Servo time constant $T_g$ | 0.30 s |
| Secondary integral gain $K_i$ | 0.10 1/s |
| Initial guide vane $u_0$ | 0.53848 pu |
| Minimum / maximum gate | 0.05 / 1.00 pu |

The initial flow estimate is

$$
Q_0 = 22.1375\ \mathrm{m^3/s}.
$$

The initial surge level is not an arbitrary elevation. It is derived from the headrace-junction pressure after the initial Darcy loss:

$$
Z_{s,0}
=
H_{gross}-\frac{\Delta p_{f,headrace}}{\rho g}
=
370.975924\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

This consistent datum is important: using `69.97 m` with the present pressure convention creates an artificial pressure mismatch of roughly 301 m and destabilizes the warm-up.

## Core equations

### Penstock/headrace

HydroPowerDynamics.jl uses the one-dimensional momentum balance

$$
\frac{d\dot m}{dt}
=
\frac{A}{L}\left(p_{in}-p_{out}-\Delta p_f\right),
$$

with Darcy-Weisbach friction.

### Surge tank

$$
A_t\frac{dZ}{dt}=\frac{\dot m_s}{\rho},
$$

$$
p_j=p_{atm}+\rho gZ-\Delta p_{riser}.
$$

### Francis turbine

$$
Q=u_gK_qD^2\sqrt{|H|},
$$

$$
\eta=\eta_{max}\left[1-c_\eta\left(\frac{Q}{Q_r}-1\right)^2\right],
$$

$$
P_m=\rho gQ|H|\eta.
$$

### Rotating mass and electrical load

`RotationalPort.tau` is defined positive **into** a component. Therefore the turbine driver exports negative connector torque, while the generator/load absorbs positive connector torque. With the corrected sign convention, the rotor balance is

$$
J\dot\omega
=\tau_{r,t}+\tau_{r,g}-b\omega,
$$

where the connected turbine-side rotor torque is positive and the generator-side rotor torque is negative during normal generation.

This sign convention was explicitly corrected during validation; the previous inconsistent convention could produce a numerically successful but physically impossible rotor runaway.

## Validated results

| Metric | Without surge tank | With surge tank |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pre-step frequency range | 0.000000004 Hz | 0.000083858 Hz |
| Pre-step mechanical-power range | 0.000000004 MW | 0.000642 MW |
| Frequency nadir | **48.082640 Hz** | **48.165938 Hz** |
| Nadir time | 7.58 s | 7.86 s |
| Frequency at 65 s | 49.829378 Hz | 49.753102 Hz |
| Mechanical power at 65 s | 89.770185 MW | 89.662782 MW |
| Electrical power at 65 s | 89.692880 MW | 89.555584 MW |
| Frequency IAE | 43.744264 Hz s | 43.798963 Hz s |
| ±0.05 Hz settling by 65 s | No | No |

The surge-tank case improves the frequency nadir by

$$
\Delta f_{nadir}
=48.165938-48.082640
=\boxed{+0.083298\ \mathrm{Hz}},
$$

and moves the nadir about `0.28 s` later.

However, the surge case does not dominate every metric. At `65 s` its frequency is lower and its IAE is slightly larger. In this controller tuning, the surge tank improves the **early hydraulic/frequency transient** while also changing the slower recovery trajectory. This is exactly why the hydraulic states should be retained instead of replacing the plant by one static turbine gain.

The secondary AGC is deliberately conservative and has not restored exactly 50 Hz by 65 s. Controller retuning or a longer simulation horizon is a separate study from the surge/no-surge comparison.

## Interpretation limits

The frequency trace is the response of this isolated low-inertia benchmark plant/load model. The approximately 48.1 Hz nadir must **not** be interpreted as a prediction of Nordic-system frequency following a 15 MW event. A Nordic-system study would require the surrounding synchronous-area inertia, load-frequency sensitivity, FCR providers, network/tie-line response and other generation.

The useful conclusion here is comparative: for the same local model and controller, adding the surge tank raises the simulated nadir by about `0.0833 Hz` and reshapes the power/flow recovery.
